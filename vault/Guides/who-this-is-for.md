# Who This Library Is For

Most of what I've put here sits at roughly an upper-intermediate level. Not expert, and not a first introduction either. I've assumed you can already hold the shape of an argument in your head, follow a line of reasoning without every step spelled out, and tell the difference between a claim and the evidence for it. You don't need formal training in the subject. You do need enough footing to stay oriented while the ground shifts under you.

You will hit terms you don't know. That is expected. It is not a sign the material isn't for you, and it isn't something I've tried to design out by watering the ideas down. The unfamiliar word is the doorway, not the wall.

What matters more than what you already know is how you go about not knowing. I've written this for people with a self-taught disposition: you drive your own way through, you chase the threads that actually matter to you, and you don't wait to be walked from one idea to the next. And I've written it for people with some intuition for turning a read idea into their own words, and for spotting where it applies to a real problem sitting in front of them. Reuse over recall. If you can take a framework apart and put it back together around your own situation, you'll get far more out of this than someone who can only recite it back.

## How it works

The method is simpler than it sounds. When you hit a term you don't understand, open a chat with ChatGPT or another AI model and start asking. What does this mean. Give me an example. Now a simpler one. How does this connect to the thing I read a minute ago. Keep going until the idea is actually exposed to you and not just defined at you.

That back-and-forth is the whole mental model of teaching yourself. It's how you build intuition: not by reading harder, but by asking until it clicks, then asking the next question. Think of it as a WhatsApp conversation with a computer that has shelves and shelves of books loaded into it, patient enough to explain the same thing five different ways.

Then take it somewhere. Start a hobby project, however small, and use what you've been reading to build it. The project is where the knowledge stops being someone else's and becomes yours.

If you don't meet all of this yet, that's fine. The material still rewards a slower read, and you'll grow into the rest of it as you go.
