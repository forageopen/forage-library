# Founder Voice

The actual writing of a terse, self-taught founder is nothing like default AI "casual" writing. It's flatter, less grammatically fussy, and has zero typographic em-dashes (a plain hyphen "-" does that pause instead, since it's what's on the keyboard), zero hedging, zero enthusiasm markers. Default AI attempts at "sounding casual" usually just add contractions and exclamation points to otherwise-generic prose; that is not what this is. The goal is to reproduce the actual mechanics, not a vibe of casualness.

Read `references/voice-profile.md` before writing anything - it has the full rule set, the three-register split, and calibration examples. Don't skip it and wing this from the description alone; the anti-patterns section in particular is easy to violate by default and is the part that most determines whether the output reads as a person or as an AI imitating one.

## How to use this

1. **Figure out which register the task needs.** There are three. *Transactional/business/technical* asks get clipped imperative fragments and low affect. *Personal/reflective* content gets longer, run-on, less punctuated prose, sometimes deflected into third person or code-switched into another language. *Considered/public-facing long-form* - a founder's note, an About or welcome page, a guide, "explain X simply", storytelling for people who don't have the context yet - gets real composed prose: fully-formed sentences, but still no em-dash, still fragment-chained for emphasis, still zero hedging/enthusiasm/transitions, carried by concrete analogy and the odd aphoristic one-liner. Don't cross the wires - a business message in the personal register, or the run-on personal register in something public-facing, both read wrong. Check `references/voice-profile.md` for what distinguishes them; the long-form register has its own passage set under "Considered long-form for calibration".
2. **Cut before you add.** Most of the transformation is subtractive: drop articles, drop subject pronouns where the meaning survives, drop hedges, drop transitions, drop the exclamation point, lowercase the sentence-initial "I" (chat registers only). Where the source has an em-dash-like pause, swap in a plain hyphen "-" rather than typing "—". Resist the pull to then re-add "personality" on top - the flatness and the fragments *are* the personality.
3. **Keep it short - unless it's the long-form register.** In chat/message tasks, the founder's own turns are overwhelmingly one line to a few fragments; if a rewrite is running long, check whether it should instead be a short instruction plus a pasted block (the real pattern for handling longer content), rather than a smoothly composed paragraph. The public-facing long-form register is the exception - there it's multi-paragraph prose - but it stays subtractive at the sentence level: cut the hedge, cut the transition word, cut the softener, let a short declarative or an aphorism carry the paragraph instead of a connecting clause.
4. **Don't manufacture typos or broken grammar for its own sake by default.** Real typos are a byproduct of fast, unedited typing, not a style to imitate performatively. Write clean-but-terse; let the compression and dropped words be where the "roughness" comes from, not fake misspellings - unless raw mode applies (see step 7).
5. **When in doubt, compress harder.** The single most common failure mode when imitating a terse personal voice is producing something 80% of the way there and then padding it back out with a connecting clause or a softening phrase. If a sentence has a hedge, a transition word, or a typographic em-dash (—) in it, that's a sign to cut, not to keep.
6. **Use ";" and "-" the way this voice does.** A semicolon attaches an elaborating clause to the point just made; a hyphen "-" is the pause/aside beat that would otherwise be an em-dash. Both are cheap, fast-to-type ways to keep a thought moving without a full sentence break - reach for them instead of a period when the next clause is really just unpacking the first.
7. **Decide if "raw mode" applies.** Beyond deliberate compression, this kind of writing sometimes has genuine syntactic looseness - doubled prepositions, subject/verb mismatches, an ambiguous referent left for the reader to fill in. This is off by default because it makes output harder to parse. Turn it on only when the writer explicitly asks for "raw"/"unedited"/"how I'd actually type it messily," or the message is clearly for themselves or someone who already shares context (a personal note, a DM to a close friend) rather than a first-time external reader. See `references/voice-profile.md` for the "raw mode" section and examples - when it's on, one or two rough spots per message is enough, not a whole paragraph of tangled grammar.

## Example transformations

**Transactional register.** Generic AI draft: "Hey, just wanted to check in — do you think the new brand colors are working well, or should we look at some alternatives? Let me know your thoughts!"

Founder voice: "new brand colors working or need alternatives? whats your verdict"

**Long-form register.** Generic AI draft: "Our library is a carefully curated collection of high-quality resources designed to empower learners at every stage of their journey. We believe that knowledge should be accessible to everyone, which is why we've made it our mission to break down barriers to education and foster a vibrant community of lifelong learners."

Founder voice: "This is less an archive than a workspace. It holds frameworks, research, references, and working documents, kept in a way that invites you to take them apart and reuse them rather than only read them. I wanted it to stay open. Not sitting behind an institution that decides who gets to learn what, and not filtered through a feed built to hold attention. Just the material, organised well enough that a learner, an educator, or someone building on their own can find a way through it."

(Note what changed: the abstractions - "empower", "every stage of their journey", "vibrant community", "lifelong learners" - are gone. Claims are concrete and specific. "I wanted it to stay open." stands as its own sentence. No "we believe", no "which is why we've made it our mission".)
