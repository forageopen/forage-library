# Corpus gaps — Malaysian media law NOT in the COMMANDER corpus

The corpus is 18 Acts frozen at 1 January 2006. Several laws that are central to
Malaysian media, publishing, content and speech work are **absent entirely**. If
a matter touches any of these, the agent must say so in the opening lines of the
deliverable and route the point to a Malaysian lawyer — the corpus cannot analyse
it.

## Absent — high impact for media work

| Law | Why it matters | Typical trigger facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 (PPPA)** | The primary print-media control statute: licensing of presses and publications, the Minister's permit power, powers to prohibit publications, offences for publishing without a permit and for "false news". Arguably *the* core media law and it is not in the corpus at all. | any print publication, newsletter, magazine, book, pamphlet; distribution of a foreign publication; a "false news" allegation |
| **Defamation Act 1957** | The civil defamation framework — libel vs slander, the defences (justification, fair comment, qualified privilege, Reynolds-type privilege), offer of amends, apology in mitigation, limitation. The corpus has only *criminal* defamation (Penal Code s.499–500). Most creator/media defamation exposure is civil. | a letter of demand, a threatened or filed civil suit, a "you defamed me" complaint, deciding whether to publish a critical piece |
| **Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (PDPA)** | Processing of personal data in commercial transactions — consent, the data-protection principles, data-subject rights, cross-border transfer, and (added by a 2024 amendment — verify) data-breach notification and a data-protection-officer duty. Engaged whenever a media operation holds subscriber, source, or customer data, or publishes personal data. Note the "commercial transactions" limit and the journalism/personal-use carve-outs — for counsel. | doxxing claims, publishing someone's personal details, a subscriber list, a data leak, audience analytics |
| **Film Censorship Act 2002** | Censorship approval for films and film-publicity material before distribution/exhibition; offences for possessing or circulating uncensored film. "Film" is defined broadly and has been argued to reach online video. | any video content distributed publicly, a documentary, branded video, a trailer |
| **Official Secrets Act 1972 (OSA)** | Possession, communication or publication of material classified as an official secret; a certificate by a Minister that a document is an official secret is conclusive. Strict liability features. | reporting on leaked government documents, tenders, cabinet papers, internal memos |
| **Sedition Act 1948 — post-2006 amendment(s)** | The corpus is the pre-2015 text. A Sedition (Amendment) Act 2015 is understood to have raised penalties, cut sentencing flexibility, addressed online publication, and added court powers over online material and bail — verify each point. Any sedition analysis from the corpus understates the current position. | any sedition exposure |
| **CMA 1998 — post-2006 amendments** | The corpus CMA is the 2006 reprint. There have been several amendment Acts; a substantial package around 2024–2025 (verify Act number and commencement) is understood to have expanded online-content offences, raised penalties, and added service-provider duties and investigative powers. | any CMA s.211 / s.233 / licensing / content matter |
| **Evidence Act s.114A (2012)** | The publication presumption — a person named as, or identified by picture/pseudonym as, the owner/host/editor/administrator/moderator of a publication is *presumed* to have published its contents. Reverses the practical burden in online-content cases. Not in the corpus Evidence Act file. | every online-content prosecution or investigation |
| **Copyright Act 1987 — 2012 and later amendments** | ISP safe harbour and notice-and-takedown, technological protection measures, further offences and remedies. The corpus predates the entire online-liability regime. | takedown notices, platform liability, DRM circumvention, streaming |
| **Companies Act 2016** | Replaced the Companies Act 1965 (which *is* in the corpus, now superseded). Directors' duties, disclosure, personal liability for a media company. | any question about the client's company, its directors, or corporate filings |
| **MACC Act 2009** | Replaced the repealed Prevention of Corruption Act 1961 (a stub in the corpus). | any corruption allegation, s.17A corporate liability |

## Absent — situational

- **Communications and Multimedia (Content) regulations & the Malaysian Content Code** (MCMC subsidiary instruments — check `mcmc.gov.my`).
- **Broadcasting-specific licensing conditions** issued under the CMA.
- **Trade Descriptions Act 2011** (replaced the 1972 Act) — false trade descriptions in advertising.
- **Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transactions) Regulations 2012** — disclosure duties for online sellers / marketplaces.
- **Whistleblower Protection Act 2010**.
- **Federal Constitution** — Article 10 (speech, assembly, association) and Article 10(2) restriction grounds are cited in the client's own framework (`Law Legal Protection.md`) but the constitutional text is not a corpus file. Article 10 analysis must be flagged as done from memory / the client's framework, and verified.
- **Case law** — none of it is in the corpus. Every element that turns on judicial interpretation (what "seditious tendency" covers, what "offensive in character" means under s.233, the fair-comment defence) is beyond what the corpus can tell you.

## Standing instruction

When any of the above is in play, the deliverable's first lines read:

> **Outside the corpus.** This matter engages [law], which is not in the COMMANDER
> corpus. The analysis below covers only the corpus provisions; [law] must be
> assessed by a Malaysian advocate & solicitor before the client acts.
