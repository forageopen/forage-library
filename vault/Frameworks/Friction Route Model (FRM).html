Friction-Route Model (FRM)

Don't stop when the work gets stuck. Go around it, cut it, or ship part of it now.

Every pipeline hits friction sooner or later. That is normal, not a breakdown. The real question is never whether friction will show up. The question is what to do next so the smallest complete delivery still ships.

Most teams treat their pipeline as fixed. When a step stalls, they wait. When a path blocks, they push harder against it. FRM treats the blocked pipeline as a routing problem, not a failure. FRM runs in three steps: find where the friction is, pick one of three responses, then check that the response still delivers the smallest complete result before moving on.

Reroute

A different path can reach the same result without going through the blocked step. Work keeps moving on that other path.

Eliminate

This blocked part isn't essential. Cut it out completely. The delivery gets shorter, but nothing important is lost.

Segment

The whole delivery can't move forward yet, but part of it can. Ship that part now. The rest waits and gets scheduled later.

How it moves — one block, three routes, one check
"FRM doesn't fix the blocked step. It goes around it, removes it, or ships without it. The blocked step can be dealt with later, on its own. What matters most is that the work keeps moving."
